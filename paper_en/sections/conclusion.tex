\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The value of further reported cases for estimating transmission depends on the inferential target. For the current value, error falls without limit as cases accumulate. For the process mean across periods, the true variation of $R_t$ between periods is sampling variation of the target, so precision grows with case volume at a diminishing rate. Beyond the balance scale each further doubling of cases removes at most a quarter of the remaining variance. For the next period's value, no linear forecast from the current estimate has error below $(1-\phi^2)v_R$. In US influenza hospital admissions, an affine model placed the apparent balance ratio at about 37 admissions per state-week on the scale of a renewal-type ratio, although a power law without a floor fitted about as well. Random removal of admissions steepened the slope as binomial thinning of Poisson counts predicts and left the intercept without detectable change. Adding state intercepts lowered the ratio to about 17, a reduction within sampling variation. After calibration by simulation, the implied environmental variance was about 0.04 with a mean generation interval of 3.6 days and 0.013 to 0.026 with shorter intervals of 2.2 to 2.9 days. Of state-weeks, 55\% lay above the apparent ratio and about 44\% above the latent weekly balance scale. Between 41\% and 51\% of the intercept was shared by all states in a week. With independent periods and linear costs, a surveillance system gains more precision from further periods, and from further regions with weakly synchronised variation, than from further cases once its case volume per period exceeds $\sqrt{(c_0/c_1)\mx}$. The evidence on reporting is narrower. Under mandatory reporting, facility completeness changed the intercept little, and the comparison with the voluntary period of 2024 was inconclusive. Empirical intercepts of apparent statistics should be reported together with the calibration that links them to transmission and with the functional form on which they depend.
