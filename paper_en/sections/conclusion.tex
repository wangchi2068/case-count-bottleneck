\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The value of further reported cases for estimating transmission depends on the inferential target. For the current value, error falls without limit as cases accumulate. For the process mean across periods, between-period variation of $R_t$ is sampling variation of the target itself, so precision grows with case volume at a diminishing rate; beyond the balance scale each further doubling of cases removes at most a quarter of the remaining variance. For the next period's value, no linear forecast from the current estimate has error below $(1-\phi^2)v_R$.

In US influenza hospital admissions, an affine model placed the apparent balance ratio near 37 admissions per state-week, although a power law without a floor fitted about as well. Thinning steepened the slope as Poisson counting predicts and left the intercept unchanged; state intercepts lowered the ratio to about 17, within sampling variation. Calibration implied an environmental variance near 0.04 at a 3.6-day generation interval and 0.013 to 0.026 at 2.2 to 2.9 days, and a substantial share of the intercept was common to all states in a week.

With independent periods and linear costs, a system above $\sqrt{(c_0/c_1)\mx}$ cases per period gains more from further periods and weakly synchronised regions than from further cases. The reporting evidence is narrower: under mandatory reporting facility completeness changed the intercept little, and the 2024 voluntary comparison was inconclusive. Empirical intercepts of apparent statistics should be reported with the calibration that links them to transmission and with the functional form they depend on.
