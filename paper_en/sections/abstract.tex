\begin{abstract}
\noindent Estimates of the mean reproduction number across periods are assumed to improve as more cases are reported, although transmission varies between periods. In a branching process with environmental variation in transmission, overdispersed offspring and binomial reporting, the error variance of a single-period estimate of the mean is $V(m)=v_R+A/m$ for design case volume $m$, so precision gains diminish beyond the balance scale $\mx=A/v_R$. The balance scale enters the budget-optimal case allocation $m^*=\sqrt{(c_0/c_1)\mx}$, and volatile reporting adds a term that does not fall with $m$. In US influenza hospital admissions during early growth in four seasons from 2022--23, we fitted an affine model of the relative loss of weekly growth statistics against reporting scale. Under this model, the loss of a renewal-type ratio approached a positive intercept, with an apparent balance ratio of 37.4 admissions per week (95\% CI 19.1 to 71.0), although an intercept-free power law fitted about as well. Calibration by matched simulation gave a latent environmental variance of 0.040 (95\% CI 0.026 to 0.056), conditional on a 3.6-day interval proxy and independent weekly shocks; alternative generation-interval scenarios of 2.2 to 2.9 days lowered it to 0.013 to 0.026. Two-fifths to one half of the intercept was common to all states in a week. Within the high-participation mandatory reporting windows, facility completeness changed the intercept little; the comparison with voluntary reporting in 2024 was inconclusive. Beyond the price-adjusted threshold $m^*$, further periods and weakly synchronised jurisdictions improve precision more than further cases per period.
