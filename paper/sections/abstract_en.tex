\begin{center}
  {\large\bfseries Irreducible error and surveillance design for reproduction number estimation under environmental and reporting variance} % qa-allow-bold (English title block)
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Abstract.} Surveillance agencies must know what precision additional cases buy for reproduction number estimates. Under negative binomial transmission and binomial reporting, we distinguish two targets. For the instantaneous reproduction number, reported cases divided by the expected reported scale $m$ attain the Cramér--Rao bound. For the mean reproduction number across periods, the variance of estimators conditionally unbiased for $R_t$ is the sum of the environmental variance $v_R$, the reporting-heterogeneity variance $v_H$ and $A_H/m$; the first two terms do not decline with scale. When environments are serially independent ($\phi=0$), the same error applies to a one-step-ahead forecast, inflated by $1+1/T_0$ when the mean is estimated from $T_0$ earlier periods. We derive a threshold at which raising the reporting rate overtakes widening coverage, a lag-weight signature $F$ of reporting noise in the forecast-error intercept, an effective number of periods $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ limiting the precision of $v_R$, and a budget threshold setting the number and size of periods under autocorrelated environments. In 16,218 state-weeks of influenza hospitalisations from the US National Healthcare Safety Network, early-season forecast error fell with scale as $a+b/D$, with $b/a=22.6$ cases per week (95\% percentile interval 13.5--34.9). The reporting component observable through hospital reporting shares explained under 1\% of the excess intercept; dispersion unrelated to these shares remains unconstrained. Of seven influenza, COVID-19 and RSV pathogen--window combinations, none replicated, four partially replicated and three were consistent in direction only. With four seasons, the season-level variance has a relative standard error of at least 0.71. % qa-allow-bold (abstract run-in label)
